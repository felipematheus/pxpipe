# Dense-hex density sweep — Opus 5 vs Fable 5 (2026-07-24)

Answers: *can Opus 5 reach Fable's read fidelity if pxpipe renders at a lower
density, and what does that cost?*

Corpus generated by `gen-hex-corpus.mjs` (deterministic seed, 100 JSON lines,
15 probes spread evenly down the page), rendered at the three production cell
sizes — all single-page and inside the ≤1568×728 no-downscale cap, so every cell
below is a **shippable** configuration. Task per trial is the `verbatim-15`
task: find the line with an exact `dur_ms`, transcribe its 12-hex `id`
visually ("do not use code"). Transport is the Claude Code CLI, 75 trials.

## Grid

| variant | img tok | vs production | Opus 5 | Fable 5 |
|---|---:|---:|:---:|:---:|
| `5x8` (production) | 280 | 1.0× | **1/15** | **13/15** |
| `7x10` | 504 | 1.8× | **6/15** | — |
| `9x12` | 728 | 2.6× | **8/15** | **15/15** |

**Corpus is calibrated.** Fable scores 13/15 at production density here —
exactly its published `dense hex (N=15)` figure. The reconstruction reproduces
the reference battery's difficulty, so the Opus numbers sit on a trustworthy
scale.

## Answer: no density in the shippable range works

Opus 5 improves monotonically with cell size (1 → 6 → 8), confirming it is
density-limited. But it never approaches Fable:

- At `9x12` — 2.6× the image tokens, savings down from ~79% to ~45% — Opus 5
  reaches **8/15**, still worse than **Fable at `5x8`** (13/15) which costs
  **one third** as many tokens.
- Fable at `9x12` is **15/15**.

So there is no point on this curve where paying more tokens buys Opus parity.
The trade is strictly dominated: any budget spent widening cells for Opus buys
more accuracy if spent on Fable instead.

## The failure mode inverts — lower density is *less* safe

Splitting misses into abstentions (`EMPTY`, safe) and confidently wrong hex
strings (silent corruption):

| model / variant | hits | abstain | **wrong hex** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Opus 5 `5x8` | 1 | 13 | **1** |
| Opus 5 `7x10` | 6 | 3 | **6** |
| Opus 5 `9x12` | 8 | 3 | **4** |
| Fable 5 `5x8` | 13 | 1 | **1** |
| Fable 5 `9x12` | 15 | 0 | **0** |

At production density Opus mostly *declines to answer* (13 abstentions) — the
safe failure. As cells widen it becomes confident without becoming correct:
abstentions collapse 13 → 3 while wrong answers rise 1 → 6. Hit rate goes up,
and so does silent corruption.

Sample misreads at `9x12`:

```
gold 119bd402c4b5  got 119bd402cb55   (transposed tail)
gold 0e3cc61cf99d  got 9e3c61cf99d5   (dropped leading char, shifted)
gold 0c90fc5e7db1  got f91ce160d66f   (unrelated)
```

Near-miss strings like these are undetectable downstream — they look like valid
ids. For a proxy that rewrites conversation history, that is the worst available
failure profile.

`README.md`'s acceptance bar requires **zero silent wrong exact strings**.
Opus 5 fails it at *every* density (1, 6, 4), and fails it worst in the middle
of the range.

## Recommendation

Do not add `claude-opus-5` to `DEFAULT_MODEL_BASES`
(`src/core/applicability.ts:37`), and do not ship an opt-in lower-density Opus
profile — the density lever makes the confabulation problem worse, not better.
The existing exclusion comment should be updated: Opus 5 is a large improvement
on Opus 4.8's 0/15, but improvement is not the bar.

## Caveats

- n=1 per cell (15 trials, no repeats).
- Opus scored 7/15 on the original `eval/verbatim-15` pages but 1/15 here at
  `5x8`. The original pages are 908×328 with shorter lines; this corpus reflows
  to ~312 columns, which is plainly harder for it. Absolute Opus numbers are
  corpus-sensitive — the **monotonic trend**, the **abstain→wrong inversion**,
  and the **Fable anchor** are the load-bearing results, not the absolute scores.
- Transport is the CLI Read tool, not pxpipe's own request-body path.

## Reproduce

```sh
pnpm exec tsx eval/opus-density/gen-hex-corpus.mjs <dir> --lines 100 --probes 15
sweep.sh <dir> claude-opus-5 9x12 out.txt
```
